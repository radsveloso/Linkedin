Governance in Qlik Cloud:
how to structure users, spaces, and permissions without creating chaos

The guide every administrator should have before starting an enterprise migration

Most Qlik Cloud migrations start with the data and ignore the structure. Six months later, the environment is full of spaces created without criteria, users with excessive permissions, and nobody knowing who is responsible for what. Governance is not bureaucracy — it is what separates a scalable environment from a technical debt that grows alongside adoption.

The 4 Pillars of Governance in Qlik Cloud

1
Space Structure: the foundation of everything

In Qlik Cloud, spaces are the fundamental unit of organization. Unlike QlikView — where folders and access sections on the server controlled everything — here the logic is based on four types of spaces with distinct purposes.

👤
Personal Space
Each user has one. Used for individual development and testing. Content is not shared by default and should never go to production.
🤝
Shared Space
Collaboration between teams. Any member with permission can publish and edit. Ideal for collaborative development before managed publication.
🔒
Managed Space
Production. Only space administrators publish here. Regular users only consume. This is the final destination for all validated content.
💾
Data Space
For data connections and pipelines. Separates the data layer from analytical applications. Enables granular control over who creates and edits data sources.
⚠️

Most common mistake: using only Shared Spaces for everything. Without Managed Spaces, there is no real control over what goes to production — any developer can overwrite an app actively used by end users.

2
Roles and Permissions: who can do what

Qlik Cloud operates with two levels of permissions: tenant-level (the entire platform) and space-level (within each space). Confusing these two levels is the most common cause of access problems.

Tenant-level roles (Management Console):

Tenant Admin
Full control. Manages users, licenses, settings, and all spaces. Use sparingly.
Analytics Admin
Manages analytical content. No access to platform settings. Appropriate for BI technical leads.
Developer
Creates apps and scripts. Can create spaces. Default profile for Qlik analysts and developers.
Analyzer
Consumes dashboards. Cannot create apps or scripts. Ideal for business users.

Space-level roles:

  • ✓Owner — space creator. Full control over the space.
  • ✓Admin — manages members and space content. Can publish to Managed Spaces.
  • ✓Contributor — creates and edits content (Shared Spaces only).
  • ✓Consumer — views and interacts with apps. Cannot edit.
  • ✓Data Consumer — accesses data and connections without seeing analytical apps.
Golden rule

A user can be a Developer at the tenant level but a Consumer in a specific space. The space level always takes precedence in that context. This enables granular control without creating complex tenant-level profiles.

3
Publication Flow: from development to production

One of the biggest mistakes in Qlik Cloud environments is not having a clear content promotion workflow. The result is development apps accessible by end users and production apps being edited directly.

👤 Personal Space (individual dev) ↓ review and approval 🤝 Shared Space (staging/UAT) ↓ technical and business validation 🔒 Managed Space (production) ↓ controlled publication 👥 End Users (Consumers)

In this model, end users never have access to development spaces. Content only reaches them through the Managed Space, ensuring that only validated versions are available.

💡

For environments with larger teams, consider creating Shared Spaces per business domain (Finance, Sales, Operations) and a single Managed Space per domain for production. This prevents uncontrolled space proliferation.

4
Audit and Monitoring: who did what and when

Qlik Cloud maintains audit logs accessible via the Management Console. For enterprise environments, these logs are critical for compliance, traceability, and incident resolution.

What the Activity Center records:

  • ✓Creation, editing, and deletion of apps and data sources
  • ✓Adding and removing space members
  • ✓Permission changes at the tenant level
  • ✓Data load executions (reloads) and failures
  • ✓Application accesses (who opened, when)
  • ✗Granular in-app interactions (clicks, selections) — requires a custom extension
For GDPR / CCPA compliance

Qlik Cloud audit logs can be exported via API to your SIEM or data lake. Establish a retention policy and ensure your security team has read access to the Management Console without an Admin role.

Most common mistakes and how to avoid them

  • ✗Granting Tenant Admin to multiple users — create a restricted Admin group and document who has access and why.
  • ✗Creating a space per project or per person — organize by business domain, not by initiative. Too many spaces become a governance nightmare.
  • ✗Not having Managed Spaces — without them, there is no real separation between development and production.
  • ✗Adding users directly to spaces without an onboarding policy — use user groups (IdP integration) instead of managing permissions individually.
  • ✗Ignoring Data Spaces — mixing data connections with analytical apps makes it harder to control who accesses which data sources.

Where to start: governance checklist for new tenants

  • ✓Define the business domains that will have spaces (Finance, Sales, HR, Operations)
  • ✓Create at least one Shared Space and one Managed Space per domain
  • ✓Integrate the corporate IdP (AD, Okta, Azure AD) for group management
  • ✓Document the content promotion policy (who approves publication to production)
  • ✓Configure audit alerts for critical actions (Tenant Admin creation, space deletion)
  • ✓Quarterly review of permissions and orphaned spaces
